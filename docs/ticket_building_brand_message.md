# Ticket-Building Brand Message

**Status:** Approved messaging framework

**Date:** 2026-10-16

**Scope:** Product positioning, marketing, field-test materials, onboarding, and ticket-building
copy. This document does not authorize a prediction model, selection mechanism, ranking change,
or guaranteed-outcome claim.

## 1. Core idea

### Internal product motto

> **There is a winning ticket in every game. We just need to build it.**

This expresses the product challenge: every game offers multiple markets and many possible ticket
constructions. Bankroll Kings helps the user investigate the game, form a coherent read, compare
possible legs, and record what happened.

It does **not** mean Bankroll Kings knows the winning combination before the game, that every user
will win, or that a ticket is guaranteed to exist at a profitable price.

### Primary public line

> **Every game has a story. Build the ticket that matches your read.**

This is the default customer-facing expression. It preserves the ambition of the internal motto
without promising a winning outcome.

### Supporting line

> **See the market. Understand the context. Build your ticket. Track what happened.**

## 2. Product promise

Bankroll Kings is an evidence-driven ticket-building system. It helps a bettor:

1. understand the likely game environment;
2. inspect sides, totals, and player markets together;
3. see why a leg is worth investigating;
4. compare replacement legs that preserve the same game thesis;
5. build the ticket personally; and
6. review every leg and the overall thesis afterward.

The platform supports the decision. The user owns the decision.

## 3. Message architecture

| Layer | Customer question | Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Facts** | What is happening? | Lines, prices, injuries, roles, game logs, weather, and availability. |
| **Context** | What might explain it? | Matchups, pace, coaching tendencies, usage changes, streaks, and game environment. |
| **Research** | What has actually earned trust? | Governed mechanisms with their current status and limitations. |
| **Build** | How do I express my read? | Candidate legs, compatible alternatives, and transparent ticket construction. |
| **Review** | What did I learn? | Leg-level results, ticket outcome, price taken, original thesis, and decision source. |

Facts and context may help a user investigate a ticket. They do not become recommendations merely
because they appear together.

## 4. Approved copy examples

### Homepage or campaign headline

> **Every game has a story. Build the ticket that matches your read.**

Supporting copy:

> Explore the market, factual context, and transparent research behind a game. Compare possible
> legs, build your own ticket, and track what the result teaches you.

### Ticket builder

> **Build around your game read.**

> Choose a leg, see the evidence around it, and compare alternatives that fit the same game story.

### Swap list

> **Not sold on this leg? Explore other ways to express the same read.**

Alternatives must show their market, line, price, factual basis, and limitations. They are options,
not ranked winners.

### Postgame review

> **Review the ticket, not just the result.**

> See which legs held, which assumption failed, and whether the original game story matched what
> happened.

### Field-test flyer

> **See the game. Build your read. Track your decision.**

> Bankroll Kings brings market information, factual context, and transparent research together so
> you can evaluate the game and build your own ticket.

## 5. Language boundaries

### Approved concepts

- build your ticket;
- match the ticket to your read;
- explore possible legs;
- compare alternatives;
- understand the game story;
- evaluate for yourself;
- track and learn;
- no guaranteed winners.

### Prohibited public claims

- “We found the winning ticket.”
- “There is a guaranteed winner in every game.”
- “Build the winning ticket.”
- “These are the winning legs.”
- “The model knows the right combination.”
- “Guaranteed profit,” “lock,” or equivalent certainty language.

The word **winning** may describe a completed, graded result. It may not describe an unplayed
ticket as established fact.

## 6. Product implications

The message should eventually be supported by product behavior:

- game-story summaries that separate facts, context, and research;
- candidate legs with a plain-language selection basis;
- replacement-leg lists organized by how they relate to the same thesis;
- decision-source tracking: **User / Bankroll Kings / Both**;
- decision-time line, price, sportsbook, and timestamp;
- leg-level grading in addition to ticket-level grading; and
- postgame review of the original thesis without hindsight rewriting.

These are product requirements to specify and govern separately. This messaging document does not
open their implementation gates.

## 7. Relationship to existing doctrine

This framework implements the existing Bankroll Kings loop:

`Discover → Evaluate → Build → Save → Track → Learn`

It does not replace the decision-support doctrine. It gives that doctrine a memorable customer
story: Bankroll Kings helps the user turn a game read into a transparent, reviewable ticket.
